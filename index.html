How the colors are decided
  1. For each runway the wind is split into a headwind/tailwind component (wind speed × cos of the angle between wind and runway) and a crosswind component (wind speed × sin of that angle). Runway headings used are true (08 = 091°, 26 = 271°, 17 = 180°, 35 = 360°) because METAR and TAF winds are true. ATIS/AWOS voice winds are magnetic (about 8° less here), so do not mix them with these numbers.
  2. Gusts: crosswind and tailwind use the gust speed (worst case). Headwind credit uses the steady speed only.
  3. Variable wind (VRB, or a range like 240V300): every direction in the range is tested and the worst one counts. For VRB the runway colors test the full speed both as crosswind and as tailwind. The score counts only the worse of those two penalties, because the wind cannot be fully across and fully behind you at the same moment.
  4. Red: crosswind above your max crosswind (the default is a student's 8 kt; the POH demonstrated value is shown next to the setting), or tailwind above your max tailwind (default 6 kt), set under Your personal minimums. A value exactly at the limit is not red.
  5. Green: among the non-red runways, the one with the most headwind. Runways within 1 kt of each other are all green. In calm wind (under 3 kt) every non-red runway is green.
  6. Calm or variable wind, 07:00–19:00 local: RWY 26 is green, because that is the runway KCFO tower uses then (unless 26 is over a limit). Outside those hours every runway within limits is green.
  7. Grey: within limits but not the best choice.
  8. Distance change uses the POH correction: . Runways here are 2,438 m long, so distance is rarely the problem; control is.
How the score is decided

The score starts at 100. Each factor that is less than ideal takes points off. Then caps apply: a cap is a ceiling the score cannot rise above, used for no-go items. The score is the lower of the two. Colors follow the cockpit convention: green 80–100 normal, amber 40–79 caution (read the reasons), red 0–39 a limit is exceeded or a hazard is forecast. A ? after the number means a source did not load, so something was not checked. The flight score is the worst hour between departure and return, not the average.

  • Numbers in blue below are your personal minimums; they change when you change them.
  • Crosswind on the best runway (gust value), compared with your max crosswind of kt: free up to a third of it, then a growing penalty, so a crosswind right at your limit scores 55 at best and half of it about 95. With a 15 kt limit: free up to 5 kt, 2 points per kt from 6 to 10 kt, 7 per kt from 11 to 15 kt. Every runway over your crosswind or tailwind limit: cap 20.
  • Tailwind on the best runway: 2 per kt, up to 12. Over your kt: that runway is red.
  • Gust spread (gust minus steady wind): free up to 4 kt, then 3 per kt, up to 30. Spread over kt, or peak gust over kt: cap 35.
  • Steady wind: 1 per kt from 13 to 20 kt, 15 points from 21 kt up. Over kt: cap 35.
  • Ceiling (above ground): under ft 20 points, under ft 10 points, under your ft cap 35, under 1,000 ft cap 10 whatever you set.
  • Visibility: under SM 15 points, under your SM cap 35, under 3 SM cap 10 whatever you set.
  • Thunderstorm in the TAF or hourly forecast, or a convective SIGMET over KCFO: cap 10. A slight chance only: 15 points.
  • Precipitation: 20–49% chance 5 points, 50% or more 15. Freezing rain, freezing drizzle or ice pellets: cap 10. Snow: cap 30.
  • Fog risk: temperature within 2 °C of dew point with wind 5 kt or less around sunrise: 10 points.
  • Low-level wind shear: a G-AIRMET for wind shear over KCFO, a pilot report of wind shear within 30 nm, a wind-shear group in the KDEN TAF, or a model wind change of 20 kt or more between the surface and about 900 ft above ground: cap 35. Model change of 30 kt or more up to about 2,600 ft above ground: 20 points.
  • Thermal bumps (daytime, from the sun heating the ground): thermal strength is the average speed of the rising air, worked out from the model's ground heating (W/m²) and the depth of the layer the thermals stir. Under 1.0 m/s (200 ft/min): 0 points. 1.0–1.9 m/s: 5. 2.0–2.9 m/s: 10. 3.0 m/s (600 ft/min) or more: 15. Never a cap. Not added when a G-AIRMET or pilot report of moderate turbulence already counts for that hour.
  • Turbulence: G-AIRMET for moderate turbulence reaching down to 8,000 ft MSL or lower: 20 points. Pilot report of moderate turbulence within 30 nm below 10,000 ft in the last 2 hours: 15 points; severe: cap 20. Model wind near 8,000 ft MSL of 25–34 kt 10 points, 35 kt or more 20. G-AIRMET for surface wind over 30 kt: cap 35.
  • Mountain wave: model wind near 12,000 ft MSL from 240°–330° at 25–39 kt 5 points, 40 kt or more 10.
  • Alerts: SIGMET over KCFO cap 15. High Wind, Winter Storm, Blizzard and similar warnings, Dense Fog Advisory: cap 15. Wind Advisory, Winter Weather Advisory: cap 39. Other alerts 5 points.
  • Density altitude: 7,500–8,999 ft 5 points, 9,000 ft or more 10. Sun glare on the best runway, frost, below −10 °C: 5 points each.

Model winds come from Open-Meteo (a computer forecast, not an official advisory). The wind-shear and turbulence numbers taken from it are estimates and have not been validated for KCFO. G-AIRMETs and pilot reports come from aviationweather.gov through a job that GitHub runs every 30 minutes, so they can be up to about an hour old; if the saved file is more than 2 hours old the score shows ? and a tick box appears so you can enter what you saw on the map. A G-AIRMET looks only 12 hours ahead. Darkness is not scored. The score knows nothing about NOTAMs, airspace restrictions, the aircraft or the pilot.

How to read the runway boxes and the hourly table

The Takeoff and Landing boxes at the top use the forecast for those two times. Two forecasts exist (NWS hourly for the KCFO location and the KDEN TAF). The boxes show the one that gives the lower score, and name it; the other one is summarized in one line under the boxes. The number beside each runway is the score if you use that runway. Tap a box to open its numbers and the reasons for its score.

KCFO has no TAF. The nearest TAF is Denver International (KDEN), 6 nm NW. NWS hourly is the National Weather Service gridded forecast for the KCFO location, including gusts. Tap a row to see that hour in the runway boxes under the table. Rows inside your flight window have a blue bar on the left. Wind text is colored by speed in knots, like the windy.com map: 0–2 3–6 7–10 11–14 15–19 20–24 25–29 30–37 38+. The steady wind and the gust each take the color of their own number. This color shows only how strong the wind is; whether it is within your limits is shown by the runway cells and the score. Cat. is the flight category from the forecast ceiling (lowest broken or overcast cloud layer, in ft above ground) and visibility (in statute miles, SM): VFR = ceiling above 3,000 ft and visibility above 5 SM; MVFR = 1,000 to 3,000 ft or 3 to 5 SM; IFR = 500 to 999 ft or 1 to under 3 SM; LIFR = under 500 ft or under 1 SM. It uses the lower value of NWS hourly and the KDEN TAF; "?" means neither gives both. Each runway box shows its own score next to the runway number, for when the tower gives you a runway that is usable but not the best: same rules, with that runway's crosswind and tailwind. Score in this table is one number from 0 to 100 for the hour, assuming you use the best runway: tap it to read the reasons.

Does tailwind reduce the crosswind maximum? (short answer)

By the POH: no. The 15 kt figure is a crosswind component only, it is "demonstrated" (shown by the test pilot), not a structural limit, and the POH does not combine it with tailwind.

In practice: tailwind makes the same crosswind harder. In the air the airplane only feels airspeed, so a 10 kt crosswind needs the same crab angle with or without tailwind. On the ground it changes: with a tailwind you touch down faster over the ground (about 84 kt instead of 64 kt with 10 kt tail versus 10 kt head, at your 65 KIAS final approach speed here), the airflow over the rudder dies sooner while you are still rolling, and the wind ends up coming from behind and the side, which tries to swing the tail around. At KCFO you almost never need to accept that: one of the four runway directions always has a headwind and at most 71% of the wind as crosswind.
